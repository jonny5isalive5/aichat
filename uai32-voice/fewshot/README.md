# Few-shot spoken-command benchmark (preserved, speaker-independent)

Preserved on 2026-10-08 from the scratch space of the 2026-10-07 investigation whose verbatim report is
`../reports/fewshot-accuracy.md`. Nothing here touches the frozen release in `../../uai32/`; `bin/uai32` is a
byte-identical copy of `../../uai32/dist/uai32` (SHA-256 `996d73cb…`, see `bin/SHA256SUMS`).

> **The network that was benchmarked on speech is 144-16-k (5,902 bytes for 3 commands, 5,936 bytes for 3 commands
> plus a synthetic "unknown" class or for 4 commands, 5,970 bytes for 4 + unknown). It is NOT the 192-44-6 network
> (19,068 bytes) quoted by the front-end investigation.** 192-44-6 is a budget ceiling computed from the model-file
> formula (`8 + 8·NI + 2·(NH·(NI+1) + NO·(NH+1))`) for the 11,412-byte merged FFT executable; it was never trained
> on anything. See "Byte accounting" below.

Every number in this directory is **speaker-independent**: the five enrolment recordings per command come from random
speakers and all development and test recordings come from other speakers, because no speaker in the dataset has five
recordings of every word of any tested command set (`manifests/dataset.txt`). That is strictly harder than the
challenge's enrolled-speaker setting and is the reason for the enrolled-speaker pilot in `../pilot/`.

## What was measured

Dataset: Google `mini_speech_commands` (8 words × 1,000 one-second 16 kHz clips; `manifests/dataset.txt`).
Command sets: yes/no/stop (20 test draws, seeds 1–20; 10 dev draws, seeds 101–110), up/down/go (same seeds),
yes/no/stop/go (10 test draws, seeds 1–10; 5 dev draws, seeds 101–105). One draw (`fewshot.split`, numpy
`default_rng(seed)`): 5 enrolment recordings per command (10/20/50 in the sweep); then, from speakers that
contributed no enrolment recording, 100 test and 100 dev recordings per command and, for each of the five (four)
non-command words, 200 dev and 200 test "unfamiliar" recordings (1,000 + 1,000 for three commands, 800 + 800 for
four). Dev and test are disjoint within a draw; draws overlap in recordings.

Metrics per method and draw (`code/summarize.py`, `code/analyze.py`): closed-set accuracy on the 300 test
positives; AUC and EER of in-vocabulary versus unfamiliar scores; **recall at 1% false accepts** = fraction of test
positives that are accepted *and* assigned the right command, with the operating point chosen in two ways:

* **per-draw threshold**: the 11th-largest score among that draw's 1,000 dev negatives (accept if score > threshold,
  so at most 10 = 1% of dev negatives are accepted); the resulting *test* false-accept rate is then measured;
* **fixed threshold**: the median of the per-draw dev thresholds over the *separate* dev seeds, frozen before testing.

False-accept rates are reported pooled over draws with Wilson 95% intervals (optimistic, because draws share
recordings) and as mean ± sd across draws. "Oracle" recall (threshold set on the test negatives themselves) is an
upper bound only. Controls: example-level shuffled enrolment labels (10 draws) and an untrained (0-epoch) uai32 net.

## Feature recipe (the one the benchmark ran on; `code/features.py`, `code/feats2.py`, `fewshot.pooled_vectors`, `code/frontend.c`)

1. Input: signed 16-bit mono PCM at 16,000 Hz, exactly 16,000 samples (shorter clips zero-padded at the end,
   longer truncated), scaled by 1/32768.
2. Pre-emphasis `y[n] = x[n] − 0.97·x[n−1]` (`y[0] = x[0]`).
3. Frames of N = 512 samples (32 ms), hop 256 (16 ms), periodic Hann window `0.5 − 0.5·cos(2πn/512)`;
   T = 1 + (16000 − 512)//256 = 61 frames, no edge padding.
4. 512-point FFT power spectrum (257 bins); 16 triangular HTK-mel filters, unit peak, between 100 and 7,600 Hz
   (edges `floor(513·f/16000)`); `logmel = ln(E + 1e-3)` → a 61 × 16 matrix (this is what `cache/logmel.npy` holds).
5. **Pooled 144-vector** (methods A and C): DCT-II across the 16 bands,
   `c_k = sqrt(2/16)·Σ_b lm[b]·cos(πk(b+0.5)/16)`, keep c1…c12 (c0 dropped, no cepstral mean subtraction);
   smoothed energy = 5-frame box mean of the band-averaged log-mel; take the 48-frame window whose summed
   (energy − min energy) is largest (first maximum); average the 12-coefficient frames over 12 segments of 4 frames
   → 12 × 12 = 144 float32 values, written to uai32 rows with `%.5g`.
6. **DTW frames** (method B): the full 61 × 16 log-mel with per-utterance, per-band mean/variance normalisation
   `(x − mean_t)/(std_t + 1e-3)`; DTW with steps (1,0),(0,1),(1,1), Euclidean local cost, path cost divided by
   61 + 61 (`code/dtw.c`).

`code/frontend.c` is the C version of steps 1–6 (`frontend vec` prints the 144-vector as a uai32 row, `frontend seq`
the 61 × 16 CMVN frames). Checked 2026-10-08 on 40 random clips against the numpy code: max |difference| 4.75e-4 on
the pooled vector (values −14.6…3.9) and 4.25e-3 on the CMVN frames.

**This recipe is not the one in `../frontend/`** (256-point FFT, 16 ms frames / 8 ms hop, 124 frames, 100–7,000 Hz,
floor 1e-7, 192 pooled log-energies, no DCT). The accuracy numbers below were obtained with the recipe above and do
not transfer unchanged to the merged `uai32 feat` front-end; the pilot's applications state which recipe they use.

## Methods

* **A_proto_cos_min / A_proto_cos_ratio**: class means of the 5 enrolment vectors per command; cosine distance
  `d_c`; prediction = nearest mean; score `min` = −d_best, `ratio` = d_second/d_best. State: k × 144 float32.
* **A_1nn_cos_min**: nearest of the 15 (5k) enrolment vectors by cosine distance, per-class minimum, score −d_best.
  State: all enrolment vectors.
* **B_dtw_min / B_dtw_margin**: nearest DTW template among the 5k CMVN log-mel templates, per-class minimum;
  score −d_best or d_second − d_best. State: 5k × 61 × 16 templates (float32 or uint8; `results/uint8_dtw_check.txt`).
* **C0_mlp_plain**: `uai32 train` on the 15 (20) enrolment vectors, hidden 16, lr 0.01, 300 epochs, seed = draw seed.
* **C1_mlp_aug**: + 30 augmented copies of every enrolment clip (waveform shift ±0.1 s, gain ±6 dB, white or
  brown noise at 10–30 dB SNR; `fewshot.augment_waves`, rng seed + 7), 5 epochs.
* **C2_mlp_neg**: 15 enrolment vectors + 15 synthetic "unknown" vectors as an extra class k, 300 epochs.
* **C3_mlp_aug_neg**: 15 + 450 augmented + 309 synthetic unknown (232 waveform-level: time-reversed clips, halves of
  two different commands spliced, noise-only clips; 77 feature-level: segment-shuffled vectors), 20 epochs.
  Score for all C variants = largest printed softmax probability among the command outputs, 0 if the argmax is the
  unknown class. uai32 prints probabilities with `%.3f`; see `FAILURES.md` for what that does to rejection.

Hyper-parameters, feature choices and calibrations were selected on pilot seeds 901–905 (yes/no/stop only;
`code/pilot*.py`, `code/diag*.py`), never reused for reported numbers.

### Cross-check against `../reports/fewshot-accuracy.md`

Every number in the report's Measurements section was compared with `results/summary.txt`, `results/summary.json`,
`results/negsets.txt`, `results/control_untrained.txt`, `results/uint8_dtw_check.txt` and the logs. The following
agree exactly: all closed-set, AUC, EER, per-draw recall (including the per-command values and the pooled counts
875/6000 and Wilson 13.7–15.5% for DTW-margin, and "no 1.4%" for the prototype = 0.0135), per-draw test FA ranges,
fixed-threshold recalls and FA (0.46–1.54%, DTW-margin 0.89% [0.76, 1.02], proto-ratio 1.54% [1.37, 1.71]),
the up/down/go and 4-command figures, oracle recall 32.6%/43.1% for DTW-margin, the negative-set exclusion
figures, the enrolment sweep, both controls, the saturation percentages, the state and code bytes, 822 short clips
and 683–723 speakers per word. Discrepancies found (all small, none changes a conclusion):

1. Report Summary says "MLP 65–70% on yes/no/stop"; the table has 64.7–70.2% (C2_mlp_neg is 64.7%).
2. Report Summary says in/out AUC "0.58–0.76"; the table spans 0.577–0.804 (C3_mlp_aug_neg 0.804; the report's
   Measurements list it correctly).
3. Report Summary says excluding similar-sounding words leaves recall "12–15% stays 12–13%"; `negsets.txt` has
   prototype 12.2% → 11.6%, DTW-margin 14.6% → 13.3%, but 1-NN 14.8% → 15.9% (up, not down).
4. Report says the MLP at 20 per command reaches "79–81%" closed-set; the four variants are 78.0, 80.6, 78.0 and
   78.6% (`summary.json`: C0 79.0%, C1 80.6%, C2 78.0%, C3 78.6%).
5. Report says 4-command "'no' and 'go' recall ≤ 3% for all methods"; C3_mlp_aug_neg has 'no' = 3.3% (0.0330).
6. Report says "a full draw with all 9 method variants 7–9 s on one core"; the logs show 7–11 s for three
   commands, 8–11 s for four, and 14–17 s for the 20-enrolment MLP run.
7. Report says "no speaker has ≥ 5 recordings of each word in any triple": exact for the tested sets, but over all
   56 triples two speakers have ≥ 5 recordings of each of go/left/yes (`manifests/dataset.txt`).
8. Report says the C front end was built "with the uai32 flags": it was built with those flags minus `-std=c99`
   (with `-std=c99` it does not compile); the byte figures (2,006 B `.text`, 4,680 B ELF) are reproduced exactly
   with that one change. See `FAILURES.md`.
9. The saturation figures quoted together (25.9/21.3/8.2/4.6/0.1/0.3%) come from three slightly different
   definitions in `pilot_mlp.py` (score ≥ 0.999), `pilot2.py` (score equal to the largest test-negative score) and
   `pilot3.py` (score ≥ 0.9995). See `FAILURES.md`.
10. Report's DTW timing "2,600 queries × 15 templates in 2.1 s" was 1.37 s on 2026-10-08 (machine-dependent).
