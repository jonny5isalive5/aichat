# Investigation report: fewshot-accuracy

Verbatim final report of the investigating agent (2026-10-07). Paths it cites were under the session scratch space; the preserved copies are in this directory tree.

## Summary

I built a complete, seeded few-shot spoken-command benchmark on Google's mini_speech_commands (8 words x 1,000 clips, 16 kHz, downloaded to an isolated directory, sha256 49650f23...) and ran the real uAI-32 binary (copied, unmodified) against template baselines on identical features. Important caveat discovered first: no speaker in this subset has 5 recordings of each word in any triple, so the challenge's enrolled-speaker setting cannot be simulated; every number here is speaker-independent (enrol from 5 random speakers, test on other speakers), which is strictly harder than the challenge. The front end is a C-reimplementable recipe (1 s, 512-pt Hann frames, hop 256, 16 mel bands 100-7600 Hz, ln(E+1e-3); pooled vector = DCT c1..c12 of an energy-centred 48-frame crop averaged into 12 segments = 144 values; DTW frames = 61x16 log-mel with per-utterance mean/variance normalisation); I wrote it in C too (2,006 bytes of code with the uai32 flags, matches numpy to 5e-4). Recipe, score calibrations and MLP hyper-parameters were chosen on pilot seeds 901-905 only; reported numbers use test seeds 1-20 (two triples: yes/no/stop and up/down/go), fixed thresholds come from separate dev seeds 101-110, plus a 4-command set (yes/no/stop/go, 10 draws), an enrolment-size sweep (10/20/50 per command) and shuffled-label and untrained controls. Each draw tests 100 fresh recordings per command and 1,000 dev + 1,000 test unfamiliar recordings (200 per other word, including similar-sounding go/no, up/stop). Methods: (A) nearest class mean with cosine distance (plus 1-NN), (B) C-implemented DTW nearest template (absolute-distance and margin scores), (C) uai32 144-16-k MLP trained on the 15 enrolment vectors, with/without 30x waveform augmentation (shift, gain, noise) and with/without a synthetic 'unknown' class (reversed, spliced, noise, segment-shuffled), rejection by max printed softmax probability. Headline: closed-set recognition is decent (DTW 91%, prototypes 79%, MLP 65-70% on yes/no/stop) but rejection of unfamiliar speech at <=1% false accepts collapses recall to 3-15% for EVERY method, because in the speaker-independent case a stranger's 'yes' is about as far from the five enrolment 'yes' as an unfamiliar word is (AUC of in- vs out-of-vocabulary scores 0.58-0.76). Excluding the similar-sounding words from the unfamiliar set barely helps (recall 12-15% stays 12-13%), so the limit is speaker/channel variability, not rhymes. The trained MLP is the worst of the three at 5 examples on every metric; synthetic negatives raise its in/out AUC from 0.58 to 0.80-0.85 but not the 1%-FA tail, and uai32's %.3f probability output saturates (up to 26% of unfamiliar words print 1.000 at lr 0.05), which makes a 1% operating point unreachable unless training is kept short at lr 0.01. Templates improve slowly with more enrolment data (DTW-margin recall 14.6% at 5 -> 24.9% at 50 per command); the MLP only reaches template-level closed-set accuracy (79-81%) at 20 per command and still rejects worse (8-9% recall). Controls behave: shuffled labels give 36-39% closed-set (chance 33%) and 1-7% recall; the untrained net gives 36% and 0.4%. Byte accounting: uai32 model 5,902-5,970 B (3-4 commands, with or without unknown class), prototypes 1,728/2,304 B, DTW templates 14,640/19,520 B as uint8 (58,560/78,080 B as float32, over budget; uint8 verified lossless: 92.0% closed-set and 12.0% recall both ways), so all three fit the 32,768 B budget with the 9,188 B uai32 and a 4,680 B front end, but no method comes within a factor of six of the 90%-recall-at-1%-FA target in this setting. In this harder setting the miss is enormous: best recall at 1% FA is 14.8% (1-NN) / 14.6% (DTW-margin) on yes/no/stop and 9.2% (DTW-min) on up/down/go, versus the 90% required; the MLP variants reach 0.4-5.2%.

## Measurements

- Dataset: 8,000 wavs, all 16 kHz mono 16-bit, 822 shorter than 1 s; 683-723 distinct speakers per word; no speaker has >=5 recordings of each word in any triple (yes/no/stop: 0 speakers; up/down/go: 0), so speaker-matched enrolment is impossible here (counted from filenames).
- yes/no/stop, 20 draws, 5 enrol/cmd, speaker-independent, closed-set accuracy (mean +- sd over draws): DTW 91.1 +- 2.7%, 1-NN cosine 80.1 +- 3.5%, prototype cosine 79.3 +- 3.1%, uai32 plain 65.0 +- 6.3%, uai32 +aug 70.2 +- 4.7%, uai32 +neg 64.7%, uai32 +aug+neg 68.5 +- 7.0% (results/summary.txt).
- yes/no/stop recall at the per-draw threshold admitting <=1% of 1,000 dev negatives (test FA actually 0.9-1.3%, Wilson on 20,000 pooled negatives): DTW-margin 14.6% (875/6000, Wilson 13.7-15.5%; per command yes 22%, no 5%, stop 16%), 1-NN 14.8%, prototype 12.2% (yes 25%, no 1.4%, stop 10%), DTW-min 5.9%, MLP plain 3.2%, MLP+aug 4.4%, MLP+neg 3.7%, MLP+aug+neg 4.3%.
- yes/no/stop with a FIXED threshold frozen from separate dev seeds 101-110: recall 12.1% (proto), 15.1% (1-NN), 9.8% (DTW-margin), 1.0-4.4% (MLP variants); the frozen threshold lands test FA at 0.46-1.54% (Wilson e.g. DTW-margin 0.89% [0.76,1.02]; proto-ratio 1.54% [1.37,1.71]).
- up/down/go, 20 draws: closed-set DTW 67.3%, prototype 50.0%, 1-NN 49.1%, MLP 47-56%; recall at 1% FA: DTW-min 9.2% (up 15%, down 4%, go 8%), prototype 3.9%, 1-NN 3.1%, DTW-margin 2.9%, MLP variants 0.4-1.1%; the MLP+aug+neg fixed threshold came out at 1.000 (0% recall, 0% FA).
- 4 commands yes/no/stop/go, 10 draws: closed-set DTW 74.9%, 1-NN 63.0%, prototype 61.3%, MLP 52-56%; recall at per-draw 1% FA: 1-NN 10.9%, DTW-margin 9.4%, prototype 9.1%, MLP+aug+neg 5.2%; 'no' and 'go' recall <= 3% for all methods.
- In/out-of-vocabulary score separation (AUC / EER, yes/no/stop): prototype 0.657/0.392, 1-NN 0.715/0.341, DTW-min 0.639/0.393, DTW-margin 0.739/0.318, MLP plain 0.577/0.448, MLP+aug 0.594/0.437, MLP+neg 0.749/0.330, MLP+aug+neg 0.804/0.278; oracle recall at 5%/10% FA for DTW-margin only 32.6%/43.1%.
- Removing the similar-sounding words from both dev and test negatives (results/negsets.txt): yes/no/stop excl go+up: prototype 12.2% -> 11.6%, DTW-margin 14.6% -> 13.3%, MLP+aug+neg 4.3% -> 6.8%; up/down/go excl no+stop: DTW-min 9.2% -> 21.9%, prototype 3.9% -> 11.5%, MLP <= 1.9%.
- uai32 output saturation (pilot seeds 901-903, yes/no/stop): with 30x augmentation at lr 0.05 the fraction of unfamiliar words whose printed max probability is 1.000 was 25.9% (60 epochs), 21.3% (30), 8.2% (10), 4.6% (5) -> no threshold on the %.3f output can reach 1% FA (recall 0); at lr 0.01, 5 epochs it fell to 0.1%; with the synthetic-unknown class 20 epochs at lr 0.01 gave 0.3% (work/pilot_mlp.log, pilot2.log, pilot3.log). In the 20-enrol run the non-augmented MLPs (300 epochs) saturated again: threshold 1.000, 0 accepts out of 10,000 negatives and 0% recall.
- Enrolment-size sweep, yes/no/stop, 10 draws (templates): recall at 1% FA DTW-margin 14.6% (5/cmd) -> 15.5% (10) -> 21.8% (20) -> 24.9% (50); 1-NN 14.8 -> 18.6 -> 20.8 -> 28.1%; prototype 12.2 -> 12.5 -> 13.7 -> 14.3%; closed-set DTW 91.1 -> 91.8 -> 94.7 -> 94.6%. MLP at 20/cmd with 8x aug: closed-set 79-81%, recall 8.2% (+aug) / 8.7% (+aug+neg).
- Controls (yes/no/stop, seeds 1-10): example-level shuffled enrolment labels -> closed-set 36-39% for all methods (chance 33.3%), recall at 1% FA 0.6-7.1%; untrained uai32 (0 epochs, random He init) -> closed-set 36.4 +- 5.7%, recall 0.4 +- 0.3% (results/control_untrained.txt).
- State bytes (3 cmds / 4 cmds): uai32 model 144-16-k 5,902 / 5,936 B, with unknown class 5,936 / 5,970 B (formula 8+8*NI+2*(NH*(NI+1)+NO*(NH+1)), matched by actual file sizes); prototypes float32 1,728 / 2,304 B; all 15/20 enrolment vectors 8,640 / 11,520 B; DTW templates float32 58,560 / 78,080 B (over budget), uint8 14,640 / 19,520 B; uint8 quantisation (0.05 steps) verified identical: closed-set 92.0% and recall 12.0% for both float32 and uint8 over seeds 1-10 (results/uint8_dtw_check.txt).
- Code bytes with the uai32 Makefile flags: feature front end .text 2,006 B (standalone stripped ELF 4,680 B), DTW .text 546 B (779 B as a -Os object in an earlier build), uai32 9,188 B; totals: uai32 + front-end ELF + 4-class model 19,804 B; front-end ELF + DTW + uint8 templates 20,120 B (3 cmds) / 25,000 B (4 cmds); front end + prototypes 6,408 / 6,984 B.
- Run time: C DTW scores 2,600 queries x 15 templates in 2.1 s; each uai32 train+predict variant 1-3 s; a full draw with all 9 method variants 7-9 s on one core.

## Recommendation

Do not adopt the 90%-recall / 1%-false-accept acceptance condition as written unless the challenge explicitly restricts testing to the enrolled speaker (and ideally the enrolment microphone): on real crowd-sourced speech, speaker-independent five-shot recognition of three words reaches only 5-15% recall at 1% false accepts with the best template methods and 0.4-5% with the trained uAI-32 net, a factor of six or more below target, and the deficit is dominated by speaker/channel variability, not by similar-sounding words. If the enrolled-speaker setting is kept, the protocol must say so and the controls should include a cross-speaker test so the result cannot be mistaken for speaker-independent performance. For the architecture, a compact-template design (DTW or 1-NN on per-utterance-normalised log-mel/MFCC, templates stored as uint8, 14.6 KB for 3 commands and 19.5 KB for 4, plus a ~4.7 KB front end) beats the trainable MLP on every metric at five examples and trivially supports adding a fourth command after restart (append five templates); the uAI-32 route (9,188 B binary + 5,970 B model + front end, ~19.8 KB) only becomes competitive at ~20 examples per command and needs three things to be viable: a synthetic 'unknown' output class trained from reversed/spliced/noise versions of the enrolment audio (raises in/out AUC from 0.58 to 0.80-0.85), short low-rate training (lr 0.01, 5-20 epochs) so the softmax does not saturate, and higher-resolution output than the current %.3f probabilities (print logits or %.5f, a one-line change) so a 1% operating point can be expressed; also note uai32 cannot add an output class to a saved model, so the declared capacity (k commands + unknown) must be fixed at first training or the enrolment vectors (2.9-8.6 KB) kept as state for retraining. Whatever the method, the threshold must be fixed from data the device has (synthetic negatives or a shipped constant); our frozen thresholds landed test FA between 0.5% and 1.5%, so the spec should target a margin (e.g. 0.5% on development data) and define the pass rule on counts with confidence intervals: with 300 unfamiliar recordings per speaker, 3 accepts is 1.0% point estimate but a 2.9% Wilson upper bound, so either require 0-1 accepts or raise the unfamiliar set size. A 5% or 10% false-accept tier would make the comparison informative (DTW-margin oracle recall is 33% at 5% and 43% at 10% FA here) rather than a floor effect.

## Risks

- Speaker-independent only: enrolment and test speakers are always different, which is harder than the challenge's enrolled-speaker condition; the size of the gap to the real setting is unknown and could be large (single speaker, single microphone, quiet room), so these numbers are a lower bound, not a prediction of the challenge outcome.
- Single dataset with heterogeneous crowd-sourced microphones and noise; 822 of 8,000 clips are shorter than 1 s and zero-padded. Results may be pessimistic for a controlled desktop microphone and optimistic for noisy environments.
- Feature recipe, score calibrations (margin/ratio) and MLP hyper-parameters (H=16, lr 0.01, epochs 300/5/20, 30 augmented copies) were selected on pilot seeds 901-905 using the yes/no/stop words; the up/down/go and 4-command runs are the cleaner tests of those choices. Hyper-parameters were not exhaustively searched (H=32 and L2-normalised inputs were tried and did not help).
- Draws overlap in test recordings, so the pooled Wilson intervals understate the uncertainty; the across-draw standard deviations (e.g. recall 14.6 +- 7.8% for DTW-margin) are the honest spread.
- The MLP rejection score is quantised to 0.001 by uai32's printed output; some of the MLP's deficit is this resolution/saturation artefact rather than the network itself (fixed thresholds of 1.000 produced 0% recall in two runs). Still, even the oracle recall at 1% FA for the MLP never exceeded 5%.
- Synthetic negatives were built only from the enrolment audio (no outside data); real negatives are not allowed by the 'initially untrained' rule, but a shipped generic 'unknown' template set would be a legitimate design the challenge should rule in or out explicitly.
- DTW cost grows with template count (15 templates x 61 x 61 cells per query); the measured 546-779 B of DTW code assumes no banding; uint8 template storage was verified lossless only for the margin score on yes/no/stop.
- The front-end byte figures are for a separate ELF (4,680 B) built with the uai32 flags; integrating it into uai32.c would cost ~2-2.5 KB instead, which I estimated rather than built.
